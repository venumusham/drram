## From reconstruction to modern aesthetic surgery

Historically, plastic surgery focused on wound closure, burns, and post-traumatic repair. Advances in anaesthesia, microsurgery, and infection control expanded what could be rebuilt. Aesthetic procedures grew from the same principles: respect for blood supply, tension-free closure, and long-term scar behaviour.

## Techniques patients ask about today

### Minimally invasive approaches

Smaller incisions, endoscopic assistance in selected face procedures, and refined liposuction technology can reduce downtime — when anatomy allows. “Minimally invasive” does not mean “minor surgery”; patient selection remains key.

### Energy-assisted devices

Laser, ultrasound, and radiofrequency tools can assist fat removal or skin tightening. Outcomes still depend on surgeon training — the device is not the surgeon.

### Microsurgery and complex reconstruction

Tissue transfer with microscopic vessel repair enables limb salvage, breast reconstruction, and complex wound cover — core skills in tertiary reconstructive practice.

## What has not changed

- **Credentials matter** — DNB / MCh Plastic Surgery, medical council registration, accredited facilities  
- **Informed consent** — risks must be explained in plain language  
- **Follow-up** — healing and complications are managed over weeks, not a single visit
