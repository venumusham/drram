Rhinoplasty reshapes the nose for better balance with the face — and, when needed, improves breathing through the internal nasal passages. It is one of the most detail-oriented procedures in plastic surgery.

## Open vs closed rhinoplasty

| Technique | Incisions | Often used when |
|-----------|-----------|-----------------|
| **Closed** | Inside nostrils only | Tip work is limited; selected hump or bridge changes |
| **Open** | Small columellar scar + internal | Tip rotation, asymmetry, complex revision |

There is no universal “better” method — the choice depends on your anatomy and goals.

## Common goals patients describe

- Reducing a dorsal hump  
- Refining a wide or bulbous tip  
- Straightening deviation after injury  
- Narrowing the nostril base  
- Improving airflow (septoplasty / valve support may be combined)

## Preparation before surgery

Your surgeon may ask you to:

- Stop smoking and nicotine (critical for healing)  
- Avoid blood thinners and certain supplements as directed  
- Share photos from multiple angles for planning  
- Discuss prior filler in the nose (can affect timing and technique)  

Realistic expectations matter: rhinoplasty improves harmony; it does not create a celebrity “copy” nose.

## Surgery and early recovery

Rhinoplasty is usually outpatient under general anaesthesia. Splints and tapes support the nose for about a week. Swelling and bruising around the eyes are common initially.

**Tip swelling resolves slowly** — final refinement often takes **12–18 months**, especially in thick skin.

## Choosing a rhinoplasty surgeon

Prioritise:

- Plastic surgery qualification (DNB / MCh) and rhinoplasty case volume  
- Clear discussion of limitations for your skin type and structure  
- A facility with emergency support and proper anaesthesia care
