Breast augmentation with implants remains a common procedure when patients want more volume, improved shape after pregnancy, or better symmetry. Success depends on implant choice, surgical plan, and long-term follow-up — not implant brand marketing alone.

## Choosing your surgeon

Select a **board-certified plastic surgeon** who performs breast surgery regularly. In consultation you should review:

- Implant type, size, and profile options for **your** chest measurements  
- Incision site (inframammary, peri-areolar, transaxillary) and implant plane (submuscular vs subglandular)  
- Risks specific to your anatomy  
- What happens if you need revision later  

## Saline vs silicone (high level)

| | Saline | Cohesive silicone gel |
|---|--------|------------------------|
| Feel | Often firmer | Generally softer, more natural in many patients |
| Incision | Sometimes slightly smaller | Pre-filled shell; incision size varies |
| Leak behaviour | Absorbs if shell fails | May stay contained in capsule; still needs assessment |

Your surgeon recommends based on tissue thickness, desired size, and lifestyle.

## Before surgery

Expect medical clearance, medication adjustments, and instructions on fasting before anaesthesia. Arrange help for the first 24–48 hours.

## Recovery expectations

- **Days 1–7:** tightness, swelling, limited arm lifting; surgical bra as directed  
- **Weeks 2–4:** gradual return to office work; avoid heavy chest workouts  
- **Months 3+:** shape softens as swelling resolves  

Capsular contracture, implant malposition, and sensation changes are discussed in consent — not rare enough to ignore.

## Long-term care

Implants are **not guaranteed lifetime devices**. Monitor for shape change, pain, or hardness and attend follow-up. Imaging may be advised per manufacturer guidelines and your surgeon’s protocol.
