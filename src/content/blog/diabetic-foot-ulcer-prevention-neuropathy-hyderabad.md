Diabetes can damage nerves, circulation, immunity, and wound healing. These changes make small foot injuries easy to miss and harder to heal.

## Why neuropathy is risky

Neuropathy reduces sensation. A blister, cut, or pressure point may not hurt, allowing it to worsen unnoticed. Poor circulation and high blood sugar make infection more likely and delay closure.

## Daily prevention habits

- Check both feet every day, including between toes
- Keep feet clean and dry
- Wear well-fitting footwear and do not walk barefoot
- Avoid cutting corns or calluses yourself
- Seek care early for redness, swelling, discharge, or skin breakdown

## When it is urgent

Black tissue, spreading redness, fever, foul smell, increasing discharge, or a deep ulcer requires urgent assessment. Early treatment can preserve more options.

## FAQ

### How often should a person with diabetes check their feet?

Daily checks are recommended, especially if sensation is reduced.

### Can a painless ulcer be serious?

Yes. Neuropathy can make a serious ulcer painless.
