Not every wrist swelling is a ganglion cyst. Some patients who come in with a lump around the wrist may actually have inflammation around the extensor tendons, called tenosynovitis.

In this Short, Dr. M. Ram Prabhu explains why careful clinical examination matters before deciding treatment for wrist swelling.

## Why wrist swellings need proper diagnosis

A wrist swelling may be caused by a ganglion cyst, tendon inflammation, trauma, infection, or other soft tissue conditions. The treatment changes depending on the cause.

Ganglion cysts commonly arise from joints or tendon sheaths and may feel like a smooth swelling. Extensor tendon tenosynovitis, however, is related to tendon inflammation and may be associated with pain or movement-related symptoms.

## When to see a doctor

You should get a wrist swelling checked if it is painful, growing, limiting movement, recurring after drainage, or affecting hand function.

Do not assume every wrist lump is a ganglion cyst. Proper examination by a hand or plastic surgeon helps identify whether the problem is a cyst, tenosynovitis, or another condition.
