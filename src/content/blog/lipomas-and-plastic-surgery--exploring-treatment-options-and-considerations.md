A lipoma is a benign lump of fat under the skin. It is usually soft, slow-growing, and painless. Most lipomas need **no treatment** — but some should be removed for diagnosis, comfort, or appearance.

## When to leave a lipoma alone

Observation is reasonable if the lump:

- Has been stable for years  
- Is small and not tender  
- Does not restrict movement or sit in a cosmetically critical area  
- Has been clinically assessed as typical lipoma  

Still see a doctor for **any new lump** that grows quickly, feels hard, is fixed to deeper structures, or is associated with fever or weight loss.

## When surgical removal is considered

Surgery may be advised if:

- The lipoma is enlarging or painful  
- It interferes with clothing, sport, or work  
- You want it gone for cosmetic reasons (face, forearm, chest)  
- Imaging or exam cannot rule out other diagnoses  

**Plastic surgical excision** focuses on removing the capsule completely (reducing recurrence) and placing scars thoughtfully — especially on visible areas.

## Treatment options compared

| Approach | Best for | Notes |
|----------|----------|--------|
| Watchful waiting | Small, typical lipomas | Periodic review if uncertain |
| Surgical excision | Most symptomatic or visible lipomas | Gold standard; sends tissue for histology |
| Liposuction-assisted removal | Selected soft, large lipomas | Smaller scar; recurrence risk if capsule left |

## What to expect at surgery

Lipoma removal is often done as **day surgery** under local anaesthesia for small lesions, or sedation/general for larger/deeper ones. You receive wound care instructions and usually resume light activity within days.

Histopathology confirms benign fat — an important safety step.

## Risks to discuss openly

- Scar (length depends on size and site)  
- Bruising and temporary numbness  
- Recurrence if capsule is incomplete  
- Infection (uncommon with proper technique)
