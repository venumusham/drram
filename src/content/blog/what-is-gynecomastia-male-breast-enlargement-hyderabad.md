## What gynecomastia means

Gynecomastia is enlargement of glandular tissue behind the nipple in males. It may happen on one side or both sides. In many patients, the enlarged gland is also mixed with fat.

The typical finding is a firm or rubbery glandular disc behind the nipple. The nipple may look puffy or project forward through a T-shirt.

## Why it happens

Gynecomastia can happen because of hormonal imbalance, especially relatively higher estrogen effect or lower testosterone effect. Puberty is a common time for this to appear.

Other causes can include certain medicines, supplements, anabolic steroids, medical conditions, or weight gain. In many patients, no single clear trigger is found.

## Symptoms and confidence issues

Gynecomastia can cause:

- Chest enlargement
- Puffy nipples
- Tenderness or shock-like pain in some patients
- Embarrassment while wearing fitted T-shirts
- Avoidance of swimming, gym, or shirtless situations
- Reduced confidence

The emotional effect is real. A condition that looks small to others may feel very heavy to the person living with it.

## Can exercise or medicines reduce gynecomastia?

Exercise can build chest muscle. Weight loss can reduce fat. But a firm gland behind the nipple usually does not disappear with exercise alone.

If the problem is only fat, weight reduction may help. But true gynecomastia with glandular tissue often needs surgical correction when it persists and bothers the patient.

## How gynecomastia is diagnosed

In most cases, gynecomastia is a clinical diagnosis. A plastic surgeon examines the chest, checks the gland, fat, skin, nipple position, and asymmetry.

Hormonal tests may be advised in selected cases, especially if there are signs of ongoing hormonal imbalance such as delayed beard growth, lack of underarm hair, or other endocrine symptoms.

## Treatment overview

For persistent gynecomastia, surgery is often the definitive option. Modern treatment is usually minimally invasive, using small access points to remove fat and gland.

Many patients are treated as day-care cases, meaning they come in the morning and go home the same day. Return to light work can be early, but gym and chest-loading exercises are restarted only after clearance.

## FAQ

### Is gynecomastia common?

Yes. It is common, especially around puberty, but persistent gynecomastia can continue into adulthood.

### Can gym workouts remove gynecomastia?

Workouts can build muscle and reduce fat, but they usually cannot remove firm glandular tissue behind the nipple.

### How do I know if it is fat or gland?

A plastic surgeon can examine the chest and identify whether the enlargement is gland, fat, skin excess, or a combination.
