Gynecomastia is enlargement of male breast tissue. It is common, often harmless, but can affect confidence, clothing choices, and posture. Surgery is elective — but for many men it is a durable solution when gland and fat are addressed correctly.

## True gynecomastia vs chest fat alone

**Gynecomastia** involves firm glandular tissue under the nipple. **Pseudogynecomastia** is fat without significant gland. A clinical exam (and sometimes ultrasound) distinguishes them because treatment differs.

Bodybuilders and patients after weight loss may have mixed gland, fat, and skin excess — planning must match the grade.

## What surgery aims to achieve

Goals include:

- Flattening the chest contour in and out of a shirt  
- Removing the palpable gland disc when present  
- Minimising visible scars (often peri-areolar or hidden in the fold)  
- Preserving natural chest shape — not over-aggressive hollowing  

Techniques range from **liposuction alone** (mild cases) to **lipo plus gland excision** (most grades 2–3) to **skin tightening** when skin is redundant.

## Consultation checklist

Before booking, ensure you understand:

- Your **grade** (1–4) and why it affects cost and time in theatre  
- Anaesthesia plan (local vs general) and facility accreditation  
- Garment use for 4–6 weeks and impact on work/gym  
- Risks: haematoma, contour irregularity, nipple sensitivity change, rare recurrence if triggers continue

## Recovery in brief

Most men return to desk work within a few days to a week. Gym — especially chest loading — is staged from roughly week 3–6. Final contour settles over **2–3 months**.

## Why surgeon credentials matter

Male chest reduction should be performed by a **qualified plastic surgeon** with documented gynecomastia experience. Low advertised prices often exclude gland removal, garments, or follow-up.
