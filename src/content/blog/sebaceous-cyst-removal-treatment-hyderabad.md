Sebaceous cysts are common skin lumps that many people casually call “heat boils” or “fatty boils.” They can appear on the scalp, face, arms, back, chest, or other areas of the skin.

In this video, Dr. M. Ram Prabhu explains what a sebaceous cyst is, why it forms, when it becomes painful or infected, and why complete surgical removal of the cyst sac is usually the permanent solution.

## What is a sebaceous cyst?

Our skin contains sebaceous glands, which produce oil. This oil normally travels through a small duct and reaches the skin surface, where it helps nourish and lubricate the skin.

A sebaceous cyst forms when this duct becomes blocked. The gland may continue producing material, but because the outlet is blocked, the contents collect inside and the swelling gradually increases.

This is why many people notice a slow-growing lump under the skin, especially on the scalp or back.

## Why does white paste-like material come out?

Some sebaceous cysts burst or discharge a thick, whitish, toothpaste-like material. This material comes from the trapped contents inside the cyst.

Even if the cyst drains temporarily, the cyst wall or sac may remain inside. When the opening blocks again, the cyst can refill and come back.

## Signs that a sebaceous cyst is infected

A sebaceous cyst may become infected, especially after it bursts or is repeatedly squeezed. Common signs include:

- Increasing pain.
- Redness around the lump.
- Warmth or swelling.
- Pus discharge.
- Tenderness when touched.

An infected cyst needs medical assessment. Sometimes antibiotics and drainage are needed first, followed by definitive removal later.

## Why surgical removal is usually the permanent treatment

Once a sebaceous cyst has formed, the affected gland and cyst sac usually do not return to normal function. If only the contents are squeezed out, the sac can remain and refill.

Permanent treatment generally requires surgical removal of the entire cyst along with its sac. This is a small procedure in most cases.

## How is sebaceous cyst removal done?

Sebaceous cyst removal is commonly performed under local anaesthesia. This means the area is numbed with an injection, and the patient stays awake and comfortable.

The surgeon makes a small incision over the cyst, removes the cyst and its sac completely, cleans the area, and closes the wound. Antibiotics may be prescribed when there is infection or risk of infection.

Plastic surgery planning is helpful because incision placement, careful tissue handling, and closure technique can reduce visible scarring, especially for cysts on the face, scalp or exposed areas.

## Should you squeeze a sebaceous cyst at home?

It is better not to squeeze or puncture a cyst at home. Squeezing can rupture the cyst wall, push contents deeper into tissue, increase inflammation, and raise the risk of infection.

If a lump is growing, painful, red, repeatedly discharging, or cosmetically bothersome, a consultation is safer than repeated home drainage.

## Key takeaway

Sebaceous cysts form because the oil gland duct gets blocked. They may slowly grow, burst, discharge paste-like material, or become infected.

For a permanent cure, the cyst sac usually needs to be removed surgically. A plastic surgeon can plan removal with attention to comfort, complete excision, and cosmetic healing.

## FAQs

### Is a sebaceous cyst dangerous?

Most sebaceous cysts are benign, but they can grow, rupture, become painful, or get infected. A doctor should assess any lump that is increasing in size, painful, red, or repeatedly discharging.

### Can a sebaceous cyst go away by itself?

Sometimes swelling may reduce temporarily after discharge, but the cyst can return if the cyst sac remains inside.

### What is the permanent treatment for sebaceous cyst?

Permanent treatment usually requires surgical removal of the entire cyst and its sac.

### Is sebaceous cyst removal painful?

Most small cyst removals are done under local anaesthesia. The area is numbed, so the procedure is usually comfortable.

### Can antibiotics cure a sebaceous cyst?

Antibiotics can help settle infection, but they usually do not remove the cyst sac. Definitive treatment often needs surgical excision.

### Will there be a scar after cyst removal?

Any surgery can leave a scar, but careful incision placement and plastic surgery closure techniques can help make the scar less noticeable.
