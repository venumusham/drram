Diabetic foot ulcers can progress from a small breakdown to deep infection and limb threat. Plastic surgical reconstruction — combined with vascular care, infection control, and offloading — can help close wounds and preserve function when timely referral happens.

## Why diabetic foot wounds stall

High blood sugar, neuropathy, and poor circulation reduce healing. Repeated pressure on the foot prevents closure. Without specialist care, infection can spread to bone.

## Role of plastic surgery reconstruction

After debridement and infection control, reconstruction may use:

- **Skin grafts** for suitable wound beds  
- **Local or regional flaps** to bring durable tissue with blood supply  
- **Staged plans** when defects are large or exposed structures need cover  

The goal is a **stable, closed wound** and a foot that can bear weight safely again — not merely a temporary patch.

## Why early referral matters

Delay narrows options. If you notice persistent breakdown, increasing discharge, fever, or malodour, seek urgent care. Early plastic and diabetic foot teamwork improves salvage rates.

## Beyond the wound

Restoring mobility and preventing recurrence require:

- Orthopaedic/offloading devices  
- Glycaemic control with your physician  
- Patient education on footwear and daily foot checks
