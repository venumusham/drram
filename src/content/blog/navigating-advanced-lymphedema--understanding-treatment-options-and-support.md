Lymphedema is chronic swelling from impaired lymphatic drainage. Early stages often respond well to conservative care. **Advanced lymphedema** needs coordinated medical, rehabilitative, and sometimes surgical input.

## Conservative care (foundation of treatment)

Most patients benefit from:

- **Manual lymphatic drainage (MLD)** by trained therapists  
- **Compression garments** worn consistently  
- **Skin care** to prevent infection in swollen limbs  
- **Exercise** prescribed for your stage — movement helps pump fluid when done correctly  

Consistency matters more than any single “miracle” device.

## When surgery is discussed

Selected patients may be offered procedures intended to improve drainage or reduce lymph burden — for example lymphovenous anastomosis or vascularised lymph node transfer. These are **not** for everyone; evaluation includes imaging, stage, and prior therapy response.

Surgery is usually considered after optimised conservative treatment, not as a first step.

## Self-care habits that help

- Maintain healthy weight  
- Treat skin breaks promptly  
- Avoid tight jewellery or blood pressure cuffs on affected limbs when advised  
- Plan travel and heat exposure with your therapist’s guidance  

## Emotional and practical support

Chronic swelling affects daily life. Counselling, peer groups, and clear education from your team improve adherence and quality of life.
