A tummy tuck (**abdominoplasty**) removes excess abdominal skin and tightens the muscle layer that can separate after pregnancy or major weight loss. The result is a flatter, firmer midsection — with a trade-off: a permanent lower-abdominal scar.

## What abdominoplasty treats

Abdominoplasty addresses:

- **Skin laxity** that does not shrink after weight loss  
- **Rectus diastasis** (gap between the “six-pack” muscles)  
- **Lower abdominal fullness** that persists despite fitness  

It does **not** replace weight loss. Ideal candidates are usually at a stable weight and finished with planned pregnancies.

## Benefits patients often report

- Clothes fit more smoothly at the waist  
- Improved core support (some note less back fatigue)  
- Restoration of confidence after pregnancy or bariatric journey  

## How the procedure is planned

Incision length depends on how much skin must be removed. The belly button often requires repositioning. Liposuction may be combined for flanks.

Your surgeon should explain:

- Scar placement (typically low, hidden by underwear)  
- Drain use (if any) and garment schedule  
- Expected time off work and exercise progression  

## Recovery overview

| When | Typical guidance |
|------|------------------|
| Week 1 | Bent posture, limited standing straight, no lifting |
| Weeks 2–4 | Gradual upright posture; light activity |
| Weeks 6–8+ | Staged return to core training per clearance |

Individual healing varies. Follow your surgeon’s protocol.

## Tummy tuck vs liposuction alone

- **Liposuction** — fat only; skin retracts only if elasticity is good  
- **Tummy tuck** — skin + muscle; necessary when laxity is the main issue
