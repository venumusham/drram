Accessory breast tissue, sometimes called axillary breast tissue, can appear as a persistent underarm bulge. It may be more noticeable with fitted clothing, arm movement, weight changes, or hormonal changes.

## What causes an underarm breast bulge

The area may contain fat, true accessory breast tissue, or a combination. A consultation distinguishes it from enlarged lymph nodes, cysts, and other underarm swellings.

## Treatment options

Treatment is based on tissue type and skin quality. Some patients may benefit from liposuction, while glandular accessory breast tissue may require excision. The aim is a smoother contour with a scar placed as discreetly as possible.

## Recovery

Temporary swelling, bruising, and limited arm movement are common. Garment use and return to exercise depend on the extent of surgery.

## FAQ

### Is axillary breast tissue dangerous?

It is often benign, but a new or changing underarm lump should be clinically evaluated.

### Will there be a scar?

Excision requires an incision. Its placement and length depend on the amount of tissue and skin excess.
