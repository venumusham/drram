Endoscopic gynecomastia surgery is often described as scarless because the access points can be hidden away from the areola or placed in less visible areas. The better term is minimal-scar or hidden-scar surgery.

## What endoscopic surgery changes

Traditional gynecomastia surgery often uses a small incision at the areola edge for gland removal. Endoscopic techniques use a camera and instruments through remote access points, helping avoid a visible areolar scar in selected patients.

The aim remains the same: remove the gland, contour the fat, and create a natural male chest.

## Who may be suitable

Suitability depends on grade, gland size, skin excess, fat distribution, and previous surgery. Patients with significant loose skin or very large gland may need a different approach.

## Why technique alone is not enough

A hidden scar does not guarantee a good chest contour. Good results require correct diagnosis, symmetrical gland removal, liposuction blending, bleeding control, compression, and follow-up.

## Questions to ask in consultation

Ask whether your case needs gland excision, liposuction, skin tightening, or a combined approach. Also ask where scars will be placed and what recovery restrictions apply.

## FAQ

### Is scarless gynecomastia truly scarless?

No surgery is completely scarless. The goal is to hide scars in less visible locations.

### Is endoscopic surgery better for everyone?

No. It is best for selected patients. The safest approach is based on anatomy and grade.

### Does endoscopic surgery remove the gland?

Yes, when planned correctly, gland removal can be performed with endoscopic assistance.
